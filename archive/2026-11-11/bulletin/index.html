This is Pringital News, the Living Newspaper. Your New Delhi bulletin for Wednesday, the eleventh of November, twenty twenty-six.

First, the headlines.
Prime Minister Modi and President Zelensky talk peace ahead of the Abu Dhabi round.
Cancer medicines get cheaper, with savings estimated at over three thousand five hundred crore rupees a year.
And Delhi's air remains very poor, though it eased slightly on Tuesday.

Now the news in detail.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has spoken with Prime Minister Narendra Modi ahead of renewed, US-backed peace negotiations between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi. The two leaders discussed how India might contribute to the diplomatic effort, and to reopening Black Sea grain-export routes. India restated its support for a peaceful settlement. Separately, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar discussed the conflict with Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov on Tuesday.

Revised maximum retail prices for anti-cancer medicines have come into effect under the government's trade-margin rationalisation. The National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority selected one hundred and thirty-seven non-scheduled cancer drugs for the exercise. More than one thousand formulations have reported price cuts, with consumer savings estimated at over three thousand five hundred crore rupees a year.

In the capital, Chief Minister Rekha Gupta is set to transfer free L.P.G. cylinder assistance to about fifteen lakh families. And Delhi has a new way to see the Yamuna. The Namo Yamuna River Safari has opened at Sonia Vihar, offering a six-kilometre round trip to Jagatpur.

On air quality, Delhi's twenty-four hour average A.Q.I. eased to two hundred and ninety-nine on Tuesday, down from three hundred and forty-four the day before. Doctors advise masks, and less time outdoors.

Today is National Education Day, marking the birth anniversary of Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, India's first Education Minister. Two thousand five hundred and twenty-six candidates have cleared the U.P.S.C. Civil Services Mains, and can file their detailed application forms from today until the twenty-fifth of November.

India has been allotted a Hajj quota of one lakh, seventy-five thousand and twenty-five pilgrims for twenty twenty-seven. And former Bangladesh Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina has applied for a new passport through her country's High Commission in New Delhi. Dhaka has rejected the request, and continues to seek her extradition.

Internationally, President Donald Trump says the war with Iran is, quote, effectively over, and that no new bombing campaign is planned. Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian says Tehran is ready to talk, but only on equal terms. And British author M. John Harrison has won the Booker Prize. At eighty-one, he is the oldest winner in the prize's history.

In business, markets closed higher on Monday. The Sensex rose one hundred and seven points to eighty-one thousand one hundred and six, and the Nifty ended at twenty-five thousand and forty-four. Public sector banks led the gains, with State Bank of India the top contributor.

In sport, Niveditha Nair won bronze in the women's twenty-five metre standard pistol at the I.S.S.F. World Championship in Doha, and India's women's team took silver.

And finally, a note for your day. Banks are open in Delhi today, but closed in Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Sikkim, Manipur and Himachal Pradesh for Bhai Dooj and other festivals.
